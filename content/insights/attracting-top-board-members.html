Attracting high-performing board members is essential for achieving strategic objectives and maximizing value. A board can be a strategic asset that improves performance, but attracting top talent can be challenging, and it is crucial to develop a thoughtful and strategic approach to attracting and retaining it.

Studies suggest that compensation is lower on the list for most high-performing board members. The more important factors are management quality, an opportunity to learn, and the strategic challenge they are interested in helping the company overcome.

Here are three ways to attract top talent to your board.

Define the role and responsibilities of board members

Clearly define the scope of the board's authority, its relationship with management, and its responsibilities. To attract high-performing board members, articulate the value proposition of the board position: the opportunity to contribute to the strategic direction of the company, the potential for personal and professional growth, and the ability to make a positive impact on the organization and its stakeholders. A prospectus that outlines the company's history, growth objectives, vision, mission, and values, and the roles and responsibilities of each board member, is critical to attracting top talent.

Focus on a strong board culture

Board members want to serve on boards that are well run, efficient, and collaborative. Create a culture that fosters open communication, transparency, and accountability. This requires commitment from both the board and management: board members must actively participate, provide constructive feedback, and support the strategic objectives of the organization, while management must be willing to listen to the board and be open to constructive criticism.

Offer competitive compensation

Compensation remains an important consideration. Board members are typically compensated for their time and expertise, and the package should be competitive with other similar board positions. Benchmarking your company against market levels, using resources such as the Private Company Board Compensation Survey, can help you determine a competitive package.

By defining the role and responsibilities of board members, focusing on a strong board culture, and offering competitive compensation, you can attract and retain the board talent that helps achieve your organization's strategic objectives.
