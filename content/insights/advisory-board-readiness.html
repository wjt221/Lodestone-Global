Advisory boards have become increasingly popular as a way for companies to benefit from the advice and expertise of a group of external advisors without the formal governance and legal obligations of a board of directors. Unlike a board of directors, an advisory board has no legal authority or decision-making power; it provides non-binding advice and guidance to the company's leadership team.

But how do you know if your company is ready for an advisory board, and does the size of your company matter?

Assessing readiness

CEOs and owners often believe there is some magical revenue threshold they must reach to make building a board worthwhile. We assess readiness differently. Here are the indicators that a company is ready for a high-performance advisory board.

  1. Critical strategic issues. Does the company have critical strategic issues and objectives that would benefit from experience and guidance? Is the business in a growth stage, entering new markets, introducing new products, or navigating a succession from one generation to the next? These issues are not necessarily tied to size, although complexity tends to increase as companies grow.

  2. A clear vision and strategy. Before implementing an advisory board, you need the foundational building blocks — values, mission, vision, strategic objectives. This lets the board provide focused advice, and it is also one of the best ways to attract talented members, who are often more concerned with where the company is headed and how they can help than with compensation.

  3. A willingness to listen. Owners often fear a loss of control when they hear the word "board." With an advisory board that concern is smaller than with a board of directors, but all critical stakeholders must still be willing to listen to feedback and act on it. It has to be a priority.

  4. A clear understanding of the goal. Whether it is industry expertise, connections, or strategic advice, the company should have a clear idea of what it wants to get out of the relationship.

Preparing for the board

Once a company has determined that it is ready, several steps help prepare for the relationship:

  • Define the role of the board — what it will do, which areas it will advise on, and what expertise is sought, so members are aligned with the company's goals.

  • Identify the right members — individuals with relevant expertise and experience, including industry experts, former executives, or entrepreneurs who have navigated similar challenges. Take a skills-based approach, and do not forget about boardroom culture.

  • Establish a clear structure — how often the board meets, how long meetings last, and the expectations for attendance and participation.

  • Provide clear communication — communicate goals, objectives, and challenges clearly to the board.

Regardless of your company's size, if you have critical issues that require guidance, can clearly articulate your strategic objectives and where board members can help, and are willing to listen to board-level input, your company may be ready for a high-performance advisory board.
