A board is a crucial component of any organization, and its performance can significantly impact the company's success. A high-performing board can provide strategic guidance, effective oversight, and critical support to the management team. Unfortunately, many boards fail to deliver on these expectations. Some clients come to us with a board they would rate a B- or a C+. Some categorize their board as just a "review board." In some rarer cases, board members are not even speaking with one another anymore. Poorly performing boards can lead to strategic missteps, missed opportunities, and even legal and reputational risks.

So, how do you prevent these issues from forming and improve your board's performance?

Define the roles and responsibilities

One of the most critical factors in setting up your board for success is defining the roles and responsibilities of board members. Each board member should have a clear understanding of their roles, responsibilities, and expectations, including the purpose of the board, its governance structure, the organization's mission, vision, and values, and the board's specific duties and obligations. It is also important to identify the "swim lanes" that each director will play in. To achieve this, establish a board charter that outlines the board's composition, responsibilities, compensation, meeting cadence, and other operating procedures.

Recruit the right board members

The success of a board depends heavily on the quality and diversity of its members. It is essential to have a recruitment process that identifies and attracts the best candidates. Consider the candidates' skills, expertise, and, perhaps most importantly, their alignment with the organization's mission, values, and strategic direction. It is also important to ensure that the board is diverse and inclusive. Diversity brings a range of perspectives and ideas that can help the board make better decisions, across gender, ethnicity, age, and professional background. Our research suggests that diverse boards have a more positive impact on company performance.

Develop a strong board culture

A strong board culture fosters open communication, trust, respect, and accountability among the board members. Encourage board members to express their views openly and honestly, respect each other's opinions, and hold each other accountable for their actions. As a third party, we can help address interpersonal challenges and prevent a "shadow board" from forming after the meeting. It is important to drive a strong board culture to prevent boardroom politics from standing in the way of board performance.

Provide ongoing training and development

Board members need to keep up to date with emerging trends, best practices, and legal and regulatory requirements. Provide regular sessions on governance, strategy, finance, risk management, and leadership, and offer opportunities to attend conferences, seminars, and workshops that focus on their specific areas of expertise.

Conduct board evaluations

A 360 board evaluation is a powerful tool for improving board performance. Board members, executives, and stakeholders provide feedback on each board member's performance, including their own. This multi-perspective approach provides a comprehensive and balanced view of the board's effectiveness, and can be conducted internally or by an external consultant to ensure objectivity and confidentiality. The most important step is to use the feedback to identify areas for improvement, establish action plans, and monitor progress over time.

Set an optimal agenda

Setting an optimal agenda ensures that the board is focused on the most important issues and uses its time effectively. One of the largest challenges we see is boards focusing too much on prior-quarter financials and KPIs rather than the critical issues that drive the strategic objectives of the business. Often, 80 to 90% of the meeting is dedicated to management presentations, financial analysis, and risk reviews. Instead, the majority of time should be dedicated to strategic discussions that focus on what really matters. This can be achieved by sending the board books out in advance and holding a pre-call that addresses any financial or risk questions, freeing up meeting time for more strategic discussion.

A high-performing board can be a strategic asset for any organization. To improve your board's performance, define the roles and responsibilities of board members, recruit the right members, develop a strong board culture, provide ongoing training and development, conduct regular evaluations, and set an optimal agenda. By doing so, your board can be well prepared, fully engaged, and able to make informed decisions that support the organization's success.
